LLM Study GuidesHub
A beginner's course in eight parts

Learn how language models work and how to build with them

Eight guides, written in plain language, each with diagrams, small interactive playgrounds, a quiz, and its own glossary. No maths, no prior experience needed. Start at Part 1 and go in order, or jump to what you need.

Section 1

How to use these guides

  • Read in order the first time. Each part uses ideas from the parts before it. The map below shows the dependencies.
  • Touch the playgrounds. Every guide has small interactive boxes. Changing a slider teaches more than reading about it.
  • Open the "Go deeper" panels when you are curious. They hold extra detail you can skip on a first pass.
  • Use the glossaries. Every technical word is explained the first time it appears and again in the glossary at the end of each guide. This page has a master glossary of the most important terms across the whole series.
  • Do the quiz. Six questions at the end of each part. If you miss one, the explanation tells you which step to re-read.
The picture to keep in mind

Building with a language model is like hiring a brilliant, very fast, very well-read new colleague who has no memory, cannot leave the office, and sometimes makes things up with total confidence. Parts 1 to 4 teach you how this colleague thinks and how to give them instructions, documents, and tools. Part 5 teaches you how to check their work. Parts 6 to 8 teach you how to train them further, keep them safe, and let real customers depend on them.

The map

Eight parts in three stages. Arrows show which parts build on which.

Key idea

Part 5, evaluation, sits in the middle on purpose. Once you can measure quality, every later decision (should I fine-tune, is this guardrail worth it, can I switch to a cheaper model) becomes a comparison of numbers instead of an argument.

Section 4

A two-week study plan

About an hour a day. Reading plus a small exercise, so the ideas stick.

Days 1 and 2

Part 1. Exercise: paste a paragraph into the token estimator and guess the token count before you look.

Days 3 and 4

Part 2. Exercise: rewrite a vague prompt you have used before with the prompt builder, then test both versions in any chat assistant.

Days 5 and 6

Part 3. Exercise: chunk a real document with the chunker and decide on a chunk size, then write down why.

Day 7

Part 4. Exercise: design a tool definition for something you would want an assistant to do for you.

Days 8 and 9

Part 5. Exercise: write 20 test questions with expected answers for a task you care about. This becomes your first eval set.

Day 10

Part 6. Exercise: decide, for your task, whether prompting, RAG, or fine-tuning fits, using the decision flow.

Days 11 and 12

Part 7. Exercise: list every place untrusted text could enter your imagined app, and one defence for each.

Days 13 and 14

Part 8. Exercise: fill in the launch checklist for your imagined app and estimate its monthly cost with the estimator.

Section 5

Master glossary

The forty terms you will meet most often, in plain words. Each guide has a fuller glossary of its own.

Agent
A model that works in a loop: decides on an action, uses a tool, looks at the result, and repeats until the task is done. Part 4.
Alignment
The effort to make a model behave the way people intend: helpful, honest, and not harmful. Part 7.
API
The way your code talks to a model hosted by a provider: send a request, get a response. Part 8.
Attention
The part of a model that lets each word look at the other words and decide which ones matter. Part 1.
Benchmark
A public, fixed test used to compare models fairly. Part 5.
Chain-of-thought
Asking the model to reason step by step before giving a final answer. Part 2.
Chunk
A small piece of a document, sized to fit in a prompt and to be searchable. Part 3.
Context window
The maximum amount of text (in tokens) a model can consider at once: instructions, conversation, documents, and its own answer. Part 1.
Embedding
A list of numbers representing the meaning of a piece of text, so similar meanings are close together. Part 3.
Eval
A repeatable test that measures how well a model or app does a job. Part 5.
Fine-tuning
Continuing a model's training on your own examples to shift its behaviour. Part 6.
Few-shot
Including a few worked examples in the prompt to show the model what you want. Part 2.
Function calling
The mechanism where a model asks your code to run a function and waits for the result. Also called tool use. Part 4.
Grader
The rule, model, or person that scores one model output in an eval. Part 5.
Guardrail
A check that runs before or after the model to block bad inputs or outputs. Part 7.
Hallucination
A fluent, confident statement from the model that is not true. Part 1.
Inference
Running a trained model to get an answer, as opposed to training it. Part 1.
Jailbreak
An attempt to talk a model out of its own rules. Part 7.
Latency
How long a request takes. Time to first token is how long until the first word appears. Part 8.
LLM
Large language model: a program trained on huge amounts of text to predict the next token. Part 1.
LLM-as-a-judge
Using a strong model to grade another model's answers against a rubric. Part 5.
LoRA
A cheap way to fine-tune by training small add-on layers instead of the whole model. Part 6.
MCP
Model Context Protocol: a standard way to connect tools and data sources to any model. Part 4.
Metric
A single summary number from an eval, such as accuracy or win rate. Part 5.
Observability
Logging and dashboards that let you see what your app did and why. Part 8.
Open-weight model
A model whose trained weights are published so you can run it on your own hardware. Part 1.
Parameters
The billions of numbers inside a model that were tuned during training. Also called weights. Part 1.
Prompt
All the text the model sees when it answers: system instructions, conversation, and documents. Part 2.
Prompt caching
Reusing the processed form of a long, unchanging prompt prefix to save time and money. Part 8.
Prompt injection
Text hidden in content the app reads that tries to hijack the model's instructions. Part 7.
RAG
Retrieval-augmented generation: search your documents first, then answer using what was found. Part 3.
Red teaming
Deliberately attacking your own system to find weaknesses before others do. Part 7.
Reranking
A second, more careful pass that reorders search results by relevance. Part 3.
RLHF
Reinforcement learning from human feedback: training a model on which answers people prefer. Part 1.
Streaming
Receiving the model's answer token by token as it is generated instead of waiting for the whole thing. Part 8.
System prompt
The standing instructions an app gives the model before the user says anything. Part 2.
Temperature
A setting that controls how random the model's word choices are. Lower is more predictable. Part 1.
Token
The unit a model reads and writes: roughly three quarters of an English word. Part 1.
Tool
A function the app makes available to the model, such as search, a calculator, or a database query. Part 4.
Vector database
A store designed to find the embeddings closest to a query quickly. Part 3.
